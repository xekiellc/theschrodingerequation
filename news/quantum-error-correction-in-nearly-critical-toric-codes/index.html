Topological codes near quantum criticality retain finite error-correction thresholds

A preprint argues that toric codes pushed toward phase transitions can still be efficiently decoded using bare stabilizer measurements.

November 3, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv examines whether quantum error correction remains viable when a topological code is deformed far from its ideal stabilizer limit — specifically, as it approaches a quantum phase transition into a trivial phase. The authors study the transverse-field toric code (TFTC), a model in which a tunable transverse field drives strong quantum fluctuations among the anyonic excitations that ordinarily signal errors. Despite this added complexity, the paper claims that measuring the standard, "bare" toric code stabilizers is sufficient to construct decoding algorithms that maintain a finite error threshold even when the transverse field is tuned to within an infinitesimal distance of the quantum critical point.

The central technical lever is the stoquastic nature of the TFTC Hamiltonian — a property that permits the ground states to be described in terms of a classical lattice gauge theory with non-negative weights. The authors use this structure to argue, first, that stabilizer measurements act approximately unitarily on the undecohered ground states, and second, that the optimal post-measurement decoder can be implemented efficiently via Monte Carlo simulation of a constrained lattice gauge theory. For phase-flip decoherence specifically, they report a proof that the decoding threshold is entirely independent of transverse field strength across the topological phase — a notably strong claim if it survives peer review.

The work addresses a practically relevant gap: real devices will inevitably operate away from idealized stabilizer fixed points, so understanding how error-correction capacity degrades toward phase boundaries matters for fault-tolerance engineering. The authors provide numerical evidence for a finite threshold near the critical point and theoretical (non-rigorous) arguments that stabilizer measurement is an optimal strategy in this setting. As an unreviewed preprint, the results — particularly the non-rigorous claims and numerical thresholds — should be treated as preliminary until independently scrutinized.
