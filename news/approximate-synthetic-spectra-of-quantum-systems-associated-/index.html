Synthetic spectra framework links quantum observables to classical phase-space geometry

A preprint explores how noncommuting quantum observables can approximate classical phase-space structure via 'synthetic spectra' and AMU states.

November 3, 2026

A new preprint on arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — investigates a foundational tension in quantum mechanics: how can noncommuting observables, such as position and momentum or angular momentum components, still encode the geometry of a classical phase space? The authors work within the framework of approximately macroscopically unique (AMU) states, a concept probing when quantum systems admit near-classical descriptions, building on earlier results by Lin and Wang and a question posed by Mumford.

The paper introduces and analyzes synthetic spectra — joint spectral objects constructed from noncommuting operators — and establishes conditions under which these spectra recover classical geometric structures. For position-momentum systems, the authors show that for sufficiently small ℏ the synthetic spectrum fills the entire plane. For angular-momentum systems, it is shown to contain any prescribed ball, connecting naturally to the representation theory of compact Lie groups. The authors then prove that highest-weight coadjoint orbits lie within regional synthetic spectra under an explicit variance bound, with function-space representations of SU(n), SO(n), and Sp(n) providing concrete illustrations.

The result, if it withstands peer review, offers a mathematically precise bridge between quantum operator algebras and classical symplectic geometry, with potential implications for semiclassical analysis and the study of the classical limit. Readers should note this is an unreviewed preprint and the claims have not yet been independently validated.
