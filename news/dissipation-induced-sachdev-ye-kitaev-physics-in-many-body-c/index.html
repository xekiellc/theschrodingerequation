Cavity QED proposed as platform for dissipative SYK quantum chaos

An unreviewed preprint argues two cavity-QED setups can realize Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev physics through dissipation alone.

August 24, 2026

A preprint posted to arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — proposes that cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED) devices could serve as physical platforms for realizing the dissipative Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, a theoretical framework widely studied as a tractable setting for quantum chaos and holographic duality in open many-body systems. The authors identify two distinct physical mechanisms: spontaneous atomic emission in a single-mode cavity, and photon leakage from a multimode cavity.

What the paper claims is particularly notable is a convergence result: despite the two setups starting from integrable and chaotic closed-system Hamiltonians respectively, both are said to flow toward the same non-Hermitian random-matrix universality class once dissipation is introduced. In the single-mode case, this would mean dissipation is sufficient to generate quantum chaos from an otherwise integrable system — a mechanistically distinct route to SYK behavior. The authors attribute this convergence to a tunable quantity they call the dissipative rank, controlled experimentally by the Lamb-Dicke parameter or the cavity-mode spacing.

The proposed experimental signature is a dynamical crossover from slow prethermal memory retention to rapid thermalization, which the authors suggest would be visible in single-atom-resolved density measurements. These are theoretical claims based on modeling of cavity-QED architectures; no experimental results are reported. Independent review will be necessary to assess the robustness of the convergence argument and whether the predicted signatures are within reach of current laboratory capabilities.
