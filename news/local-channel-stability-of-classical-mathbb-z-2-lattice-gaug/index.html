Stability of Z₂ lattice gauge phases proved without symmetry protection

A preprint derives sufficient conditions for classical ℤ₂ lattice gauge models to resist local quantum-channel perturbations at low temperature.

October 20, 2026

A new preprint on arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — establishes rigorous stability conditions for a family of classical ℤ₂ lattice gauge models defined on m-dimensional cells of a d-dimensional lattice. The family unifies familiar models: the Ising model appears at m = 0, and the magnetic sector of the toric code at m = 1. The authors work on the full spin Hilbert space, imposing no Gauss-law constraint, and ask when low-temperature states remain stable — in the precise sense of two-way local-quantum-channel equivalence — against weak perturbations.

The low-temperature proof rests on two structural features of these models: locally indistinguishable flat sectors and extended magnetic defects of dimension d − m − 1. For the intermediate range 1 ≤ m ≤ d − 2, the paper claims that sufficiently weak classical perturbations at sufficiently low positive temperature preserve equivalence to the canonical flat ensemble, with no internal or gauge symmetry required of the perturbation. A parallel result for noncommuting (quantum) perturbations is proved in a somewhat narrower parameter window, using local channels with polylogarithmic causal radius and product ancillary inputs. High-temperature states are shown to be equivalent instead to a product state.

To distinguish the two regimes without invoking symmetry, the authors introduce an optimized covariance between distant parallel m-tori: this quantity is non-increasing under one-way local conversion and invariant under two-way conversion, taking value 1 in the proved low-temperature domain and 0 at high temperature. The authors are careful to note that their sufficient conditions do not determine the full phase diagram, leaving the precise phase boundaries open. The result, if it survives peer review, would extend the rigorous theory of topological order stability to a broader class of models than previously covered.
