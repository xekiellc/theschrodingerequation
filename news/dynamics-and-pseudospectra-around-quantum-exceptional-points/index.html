Liouvillian exceptional points probed via pseudospectra in open quantum systems

A preprint examines how quantum jumps and interactions reshape exceptional-point physics when full Lindbladian dynamics replaces effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians.

November 6, 2026

Exceptional points (EPs) — spectral singularities where eigenvalues and their associated eigenvectors coalesce — are well-characterized in classical non-Hermitian photonics, where they produce strongly enhanced sensitivity and transient responses. Extending this physics into the quantum regime is non-trivial: dissipation must be treated through the full Lindblad master equation, and quantum fluctuations introduced by quantum jumps can substantially alter the picture relative to simpler effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian descriptions. This arXiv preprint, which has not yet been peer-reviewed, takes up that challenge directly.

The authors study signatures of Liouvillian EPs — singularities in the superoperator governing density-matrix evolution — using long-time averages and population imbalances as experimentally accessible observables. They apply the framework to two paradigmatic models: a lossy Bose-Hubbard lattice and a dissipative two-spin system. A central analytical tool is the pseudospectrum, which characterizes how sensitively eigenvalues respond to perturbations and reveals structure that the spectrum alone obscures. The paper argues that quantum jumps and inter-particle interactions produce complex, structured pseudospectra that differ markedly from their classical counterparts.

The claimed contribution is a bridge between classical EP phenomenology and realistic open quantum systems that could, in principle, be realized in cold-atom or circuit-QED platforms. Because this is an unreviewed preprint, the robustness of the proposed observables and the generality of the pseudospectral analysis across other Lindbladian models remain to be assessed by the community.
