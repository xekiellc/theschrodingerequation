Light-driven magnon pairs in NiO survive 28 ps, far outlasting prior observations

Time-resolved RIXS reveals bimagnon correlations in NiO persisting ~100× longer than previously measured dephasing times.

November 12, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv reports that correlated magnon pairs — bimagnons — created by mid-infrared light pulses in the antiferromagnet nickel oxide (NiO) survive far longer than prior experiments suggested. Using time-resolved resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS), the authors tracked magnetic excitations at finite momentum after pumping the material's phonon-assisted two-magnon absorption mode, keeping the photon energy well below the electronic bandgap to avoid generating free carriers.

The key result the authors report is a pump-induced two-magnon signal on the anti-Stokes side of the RIXS spectrum, with a decay time constant of 28 ± 11 picoseconds. That figure exceeds previously reported two-magnon dephasing times — typically under 200 femtoseconds — by more than two orders of magnitude. The signal also peaks around 4 ps after excitation, which the authors interpret as evidence for a delayed build-up driven by momentum redistribution of the light-created magnon pairs across the Brillouin zone. Crucially, the single-magnon linewidth and energy remain essentially unchanged throughout, suggesting that long-range magnetic order is preserved during the process.

The authors draw an analogy to higher-order quantum correlations studied in ultracold atomic gases, framing the result as evidence for a distinct bimagnon state beyond what single-magnon physics can describe. These are claims from a preprint that has not yet undergone peer review, and the statistical uncertainty on the central lifetime measurement remains substantial; independent replication and scrutiny of the RIXS analysis will be needed before the findings can be considered established.
