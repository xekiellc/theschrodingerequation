Quantum-logic spectroscopy detects individual molecule–atom collisions one at a time

Researchers report single-collision detection of rotationally inelastic N₂⁺–He scattering with full quantum-state resolution — an unreviewed preprint.

November 11, 2026

An arXiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) describes a technique for observing individual rotationally inelastic collisions between single trapped molecular ions and neutral atoms. The team prepared individual N₂⁺ ions in their rotational ground state inside an ion trap, exposed them to a helium atomic beam, and used quantum-logic spectroscopy — borrowed from optical-clock technology — to detect when a collision had occurred by reading out changes in the ion's motional state non-destructively.

Because the molecular quantum state can be interrogated without destroying the ion, the authors claim they can track isolated scattering events and determine state-to-state transition probabilities at the single-collision level. They compare their measured inelastic scattering probabilities against both quantum-mechanical and classical scattering calculations, finding agreement that they say provides detailed insight into the collision dynamics — something ensemble-averaged crossed-beam experiments cannot straightforwardly supply.

If the results hold up under peer review, the approach could represent a new experimental framework for state-resolved molecular scattering studies, potentially applicable to reaction dynamics and energy-transfer processes relevant to environments ranging from cold interstellar clouds to atmospheric chemistry. The authors identify single-quantum-event-resolved state-to-state scattering as a near-term target for the platform.
