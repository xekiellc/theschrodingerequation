No-go theorem challenges realist foundations of quantum parameter estimation

A preprint argues that 'true values' in quantum metrology are incompatible with entanglement and state completeness.

October 22, 2026

A new preprint posted to arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — presents a no-go theorem targeting the conceptual foundations of quantum parameter estimation. The authors examine whether standard quantum metrology, which typically treats the quantity being estimated as a pre-existing parameter with a definite 'true value,' can be reconciled with the broader formalism of quantum mechanics itself.

The paper identifies three assumptions common in the quantum metrology literature: that estimation targets a unique true value, that this value exists prior to measurement, and that it exists independently of measurement. The authors then prove that these three assumptions are jointly incompatible with two further conditions — the completeness of the quantum state (ruling out hidden variables) and the presence of entanglement between the probe and the measured system. Since probe-system entanglement is a generic prediction of standard quantum theory rather than an exotic edge case, the result has broad scope: it applies whenever one refuses both hidden-variable extensions and physical mechanisms that suppress entanglement at some scale.

The authors frame their work as motivating a more general formulation of estimation in which the estimated quantity is treated as a quantum variable subject to quantum fluctuations, rather than a classical parameter. The claim is that a fully realist account of quantum metrology — one in which measurements simply reveal pre-existing values — is, under these conditions, ultimately untenable. As an unreviewed preprint, the argument's assumptions and the scope of the no-go result remain to be scrutinized by the community.
