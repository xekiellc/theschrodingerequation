Dipole tilt tunes supersolid phase-transition order in trapped quantum gases

Simulations map how contact interaction and dipole orientation control whether supersolid transitions are continuous or discontinuous.

October 20, 2026

A theoretical study posted to arXiv — an unreviewed preprint — examines how the nature of phase transitions in trapped dipolar quantum gases changes as two experimental knobs are turned: the contact interaction strength and the tilt angle of the dipoles relative to the confining geometry. Using extended Gross-Pitaevskii simulations that incorporate both thermal and quantum fluctuations, the authors construct a finite-temperature phase diagram spanning unmodulated superfluids, triangular droplet arrays, and stripe states.

The central finding is that the order of the transition between the unmodulated superfluid and the modulated (supersolid) phases is not fixed. As the dipole tilt increases, the transition evolves from discontinuous (first-order) to continuous, then back to discontinuous at large tilts. The authors track this behavior through imaginary-time hysteresis analysis and show that particle number, temperature, and confinement geometry each further shift the balance between continuity and discontinuity. The triangular-to-stripe transition is found to be discontinuous at weak contact interactions and grows increasingly continuous near the boundary with the unmodulated phase.

The work is motivated by a concurrent experimental preprint and is intended to guide laboratory investigations of transition order in dipolar gases. Because neither study has yet undergone peer review, the phase boundaries and the precise conditions delineating first- from second-order behavior should be treated as preliminary. The results nonetheless suggest that dipolar quantum gases offer a controllable platform for studying tunable transition order in finite, inhomogeneous quantum systems.
