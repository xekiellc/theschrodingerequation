Hyperboundedness condition yields explicit entropy contraction rates for quantum channels

A preprint derives a closed-form modified log-Sobolev constant for KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups from hypercontractive bounds.

October 26, 2026

A new arXiv preprint — not yet peer-reviewed — establishes conditions under which quantum channels satisfy complete entropy contraction, a property central to understanding how quantum information degrades under noise. The authors work within the framework of finite-dimensional quantum channels that are compatible with a faithful conditional expectation, combining two inputs: a conditional hyperboundedness estimate and a spectral gap condition called GNS contraction.

For KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups, the paper derives an explicit formula for the modified logarithmic Sobolev (MLSI) constant α in terms of the spectral gap λ, a hypercontractivity time t₀, and the hyperboundedness constant M. The resulting expression, α = λ(1−2/q) / [12(λt₀ + log M + 1−2/q)], gives a quantitative handle on the rate at which relative entropy decays along the semigroup — a quantity that controls convergence to equilibrium and has applications in quantum error correction and thermodynamics.

A key extension is that when hyperboundedness holds in the completely bounded sense — that is, tensored with arbitrary auxiliary systems — the entropy contraction inherits the same rates in the fully quantum, multipartite setting. The authors claim this provides a route to complete MLSI constants without requiring separate cb-norm estimates. The result's significance will depend on whether the hypercontractivity assumptions can be verified for physically relevant semigroups, a question the preprint leaves partly open.
