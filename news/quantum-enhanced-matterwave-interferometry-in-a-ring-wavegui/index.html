BEC ring interferometer protocol claims 14.6 dB spin squeezing via interaction quench

A theoretical proposal combines fractional-revival dynamics with a controlled interaction quench to generate strong spin squeezing in a ring waveguide.

October 21, 2026

A new theoretical paper — posted as an unreviewed preprint on arXiv and not yet peer-reviewed — proposes a matter-wave interferometry protocol that exploits the fractional-revival dynamics of a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) confined to a ring waveguide. The authors use the Truncated Wigner Approximation to model the quantum dynamics, finding that simple nonlinear evolution to the quarter-revival point yields only modest spin squeezing of roughly −2 dB, which subsequently degrades as the nonlinear phase accumulates further.

The central claim is that a controlled interaction quench — abruptly switching on interactions after an initial quarter-revival beam-splitter operation on a non-interacting condensate — produces substantially stronger spin squeezing of −14.57 ± 0.27 dB. The protocol then uses a phase-control step at three-eighths of the revival period to rotate the squeezed quadrature onto the phase-sensitive direction, generating a phase-squeezed state at the half-revival time. A final quarter-revival evolution converts this into a number-squeezed state amenable to population-difference readout.

The authors argue the sequence offers a practical route to sub-shot-noise interferometry by leveraging operations — fractional revivals, interaction quenches, and phase imprinting — that are within reach of current cold-atom experiments on ring geometries. As with all preprints, these results await independent peer review, and experimental validation of the predicted squeezing levels remains to be demonstrated.
