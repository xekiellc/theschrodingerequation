Multinomial sampling cuts quantum circuit simulation shot costs by 1000×

A preprint proposes replacing per-shot sampling in Qiskit and PennyLane with direct multinomial draws, making cost independent of shot count.

October 26, 2026

In classical simulations of quantum circuits, extracting measurement statistics typically requires generating each shot individually — a step whose cost scales linearly with the number of shots S even after the full state vector is already known. A new arXiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) argues this is unnecessary when only aggregate counts are needed, and proposes a direct alternative.

The authors show that measurement counts can instead be drawn directly from a multinomial distribution, decomposed into a sequence of conditional binomial samples. Each binomial draw is performed in expected constant time using the established BTPE algorithm, making the total sampling cost effectively independent of S. The implementation also includes an optimization for states whose probability mass is concentrated on relatively few outcomes. Benchmarks against Qiskit's sampling primitives across up to 20 qubits and 106 shots report speedup factors exceeding 103 in most configurations at the largest shot count, with the resulting samples described as statistically indistinguishable from those of the reference implementations.

The approach targets a specific but routine bottleneck in quantum circuit simulation workflows. Because this is an unreviewed preprint, the benchmarking methodology and statistical equivalence claims await independent scrutiny, but the underlying algorithmic argument — that multinomial sampling is the correct abstraction when per-shot outcomes are discarded — is straightforward and well-grounded in classical statistics.
