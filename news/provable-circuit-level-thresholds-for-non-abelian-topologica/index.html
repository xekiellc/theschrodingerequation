Provable fault-tolerance thresholds established for non-abelian topological codes

A preprint claims rigorous circuit-level noise thresholds for quantum memories built from any 2D non-chiral topological order.

November 5, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv argues that fault-tolerant quantum memory can be rigorously realized in any two-dimensional non-chiral topological phase — a broad class that includes non-abelian models such as the Fibonacci and Haagerup phases, which go well beyond the abelian toric code that underpins most current error-correction research. The authors construct syndrome extraction circuits grounded in the Turaev-Viro state-sum path integral, a framework from topological quantum field theory, and pair them with an efficient classical decoding algorithm capable of operating in real time.

Crucially, the paper claims a proved threshold: under a local stochastic circuit-level noise model — widely regarded as the physically relevant benchmark for realistic hardware — the scheme is shown to tolerate error rates below a finite threshold value. Proving such thresholds rigorously, rather than establishing them only numerically, is technically demanding even for abelian codes, making this claim significant if it survives peer review. The authors also sketch how the construction can be extended to implement logical gates of a topological character.

Non-abelian topological phases are of particular theoretical interest because their anyonic excitations carry intrinsic computational power, but practical schemes with provable performance guarantees have remained elusive. This preprint has not yet been peer-reviewed, and independent verification of the proof and the decoding algorithm's efficiency claims will be needed before the results can be considered established.
