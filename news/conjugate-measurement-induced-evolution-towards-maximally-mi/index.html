Conjugate measurements drive quantum subsystems toward maximally mixed states

A preprint extends prior work showing how coupling conjugate variables to separate environment modes erases local phase-space structure.

October 19, 2026

A new preprint on arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — examines how a single-mode quantum subsystem evolves when its conjugate variables (position and momentum, for instance) are each coupled to distinct modes of a two-mode environment. The authors extend an earlier result, previously established in the interaction-dominant limit, to the full quadratic dynamics of the combined system.

By tracking the exact reduced Weyl characteristic function and the Wigner function, the authors identify a phase-space mechanism behind the asymptotic behavior: both functions gradually lose dependence on local phase-space coordinates over every fixed compact region. For a free-particle subsystem this occurs at any coupling strength, while for a harmonic oscillator a critical coupling threshold must first be crossed. The authors argue that, under finite-volume and momentum regularization, this coordinate independence corresponds to the subsystem approaching a maximally mixed state — one carrying no accessible quantum information about its initial preparation.

The work offers a precise, analytically tractable picture of environment-induced information loss in quadratic quantum systems, connecting phase-space geometry to thermalization-like behavior. As an unreviewed preprint, the claims await independent scrutiny, but the exact analytic treatment of the reduced Wigner function provides a clear and testable framework for follow-up study.
