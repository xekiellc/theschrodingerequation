LLMs propose quantum circuits that outperform state-of-the-art designs

An evolutionary LLM framework discovers circuits with fewer Toffoli gates and qubits across eight benchmark tasks.

October 15, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv describes a framework in which large language models (LLMs) iteratively propose quantum circuit designs while a separate evaluator scores them for correctness and resource efficiency. The authors apply this evolutionary search across eight tasks drawn from numerical computation, chemistry, combinatorial optimization, quantum simulation, and number theory. In seven of the eight, the framework finds circuits that reduce both Toffoli gate counts and qubit requirements relative to previously published best results — two metrics that dominate the cost of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

The eighth task proves qualitatively different. Rather than simply compressing an existing circuit, the search surfaces a new reversible architecture for prime-field modular inversion built around divsteps, a classical algorithm that had previously been considered impractical to implement in the reversible setting required by quantum computation. The authors report that this architecture improves the Toffoli-qubit trade-off for modular inversion, a primitive relevant to quantum attacks on elliptic-curve cryptography.

Because this work has not yet undergone peer review, the claimed improvements should be treated as preliminary. The results nonetheless suggest a methodological point worth watching: that LLMs operating inside an evaluator-guided loop can navigate the combinatorial space of reversible circuit design in ways that complement human-driven optimization. Independent verification of the specific gate counts and the divsteps-based architecture will be necessary before the findings can be considered established.
