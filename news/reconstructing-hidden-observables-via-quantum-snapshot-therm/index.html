Quantum snapshot thermometry proposed to recover hidden conserved-density profiles

A preprint claims fixed-basis projective snapshots, combined with an equilibrium equation of state, can reconstruct energy and charge profiles without tomography.

October 29, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv proposes a technique the authors call "quantum snapshot thermometry," which aims to extract hydrodynamic information about quantum many-body systems that standard projective measurements cannot directly access. Projective snapshots — a routine readout method in quantum simulators — are inherently blind to observables off-diagonal in the measurement basis, such as energy-density profiles when the simulator is read out in the spin basis. The paper argues that if a system locally equilibrates, an independently calibrated equilibrium equation of state can serve as a bridge, mapping the directly observed conserved-density distributions onto otherwise hidden ones.

The authors support their claims with numerical demonstrations showing that the method can recover energy fronts and extract a diffusion constant, distinguish spin transport from energy spreading, and reveal conserved-charge profiles that are invisible in the raw measurement record. They also characterize a practical limitation: symmetry can introduce ambiguities in the readout, and they propose that prior knowledge of the system's spatial structure can resolve these. The approach is presented as an alternative to quantum state tomography and multi-basis measurement protocols, neither of which is required here.

These results have not yet been peer-reviewed, and the analysis is numerical rather than experimental. Whether local equilibration holds sufficiently well in realistic simulator settings — and how sensitive the reconstruction is to errors in the equation of state calibration — remain open questions that independent scrutiny will need to address.
