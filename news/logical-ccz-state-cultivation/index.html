Direct logical CCZ state preparation proposed for fault-tolerant quantum arithmetic

An unreviewed preprint introduces an end-to-end protocol for preparing CCZ magic states on bivariate bicycle codes, potentially undercutting T-state routes.

October 14, 2026

A preprint posted to arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — proposes a direct protocol for preparing logical CCZ states, which enable Toffoli gates used extensively in fault-tolerant quantum arithmetic. Rather than converting T states into CCZ states through a multi-step pipeline, the authors describe a "cultivation" factory built on bivariate tricycle and bicycle (BT/BB) codes that prepares CCZ states natively, exploiting cup-product CCZ gates intrinsic to BT code geometry.

The protocol introduces two technical ingredients: index-2 lifted BB ancillas that enable selective Z-basis initialization, allowing cup-product gates to prepare two disjoint logical CCZ states simultaneously, and index-3 lifted BB memories for high-distance state storage. Decoder-based postselection is used to trade acceptance rate for output fidelity. Exact circuit-level simulations under a neutral-atom noise model — with single-qubit, CZ, and CCZ error rates of 0.01%, 0.1%, and 0.5% respectively — yield joint infidelities of roughly 1.8×10⁻⁵ at ~22% acceptance and 4.1×10⁻⁷ at ~9% acceptance.

The authors claim that at their target output-infidelity threshold, the spacetime cost per CCZ state is lower than that of conventional T-state cultivation followed by CCZ conversion, suggesting a more resource-efficient path to magic state supply in fault-tolerant architectures. These results are preliminary and await independent peer review before their practical implications for hardware implementation can be fully assessed.
