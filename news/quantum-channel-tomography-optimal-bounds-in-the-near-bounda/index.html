Near-boundary query complexity of quantum channel tomography fully resolved

A preprint closes the last gap in optimal sample bounds for learning unknown quantum channels, revealing two distinct transition behaviors.

November 2, 2026

Quantum channel tomography — reconstructing an unknown quantum process from repeated black-box queries — sits at the heart of verifying quantum hardware. An unreviewed preprint now claims to fully settle the query complexity of this task by resolving a previously open regime: the behavior when the dilation rate τ = rd₂/d₁ sits just above its minimum value of one. Prior work had established tight bounds at the boundary (τ = 1) and well away from it (τ ≥ 1 + Ω(1)), but the narrow near-boundary strip between the two had resisted analysis.

The authors derive matching upper and lower bounds in this regime for two distinct error metrics. For diamond norm error, they find the transition is abrupt: Heisenberg-limited scaling (favorable query counts scaling as 1/ε rather than 1/ε²) vanishes sharply the moment τ exceeds one by any non-negligible amount. For Choi trace norm error, the picture is more nuanced — the transition is gradual, with query complexity interpolating smoothly between Heisenberg and classical regimes as τ increases. The Choi trace norm bound takes the form Θ(d₁²/ε + min{(τ−1)d₁², (τ−1)²d₁²d₂²}/ε²), capturing this continuous crossover explicitly.

This is an arXiv preprint and has not yet undergone peer review; the claimed completeness of the complexity picture should be treated accordingly. If the results withstand scrutiny, they would provide the field with a unified, exact characterization of how quantum advantage in channel learning degrades as channels move away from the isometric (boundary) case — a question with direct practical relevance for near-term quantum process verification.
