Squeezed light may sharpen searches for QED vacuum nonlinearity

A preprint argues that injecting non-classical photon states into signal modes could significantly improve detection of photon–photon scattering in vacuum.

August 21, 2026

Quantum electrodynamics predicts that the vacuum itself acts as a weakly nonlinear optical medium — virtual electron–positron pairs allow photons to scatter off one another, an effect so faint it has never been directly observed in the optical regime. Many proposed experiments look for signal photons appearing in modes that would otherwise be empty, using high-intensity laser pulses as the driver. A new preprint posted to arXiv (and not yet peer-reviewed) examines what happens when, instead of leaving that signal mode in the vacuum state, experimenters seed it with a classical or non-classical field such as squeezed light.

The authors find that injecting a squeezed or other non-classical photon state into the initially empty signal mode can substantially improve detectability of the QED vacuum nonlinearity signature. The improvement comes from quantum-optical interference between the seeded state and the tiny nonlinear signal, effectively amplifying the contrast against background noise — a strategy loosely analogous to homodyne detection in interferometry.

The result is presented as a theoretical proposal; the authors do not claim experimental confirmation, and the work awaits peer review. If the analysis holds up, it could inform the design of next-generation experiments at high-power laser facilities — such as those planning to probe light-by-light scattering in vacuum — by offering a concrete route to relax the otherwise extreme requirements on laser intensity or photon-counting sensitivity.
