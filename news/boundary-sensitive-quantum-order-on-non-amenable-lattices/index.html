Cayley tree models reveal boundary-driven quantum order beyond Landau paradigm

A preprint reports an intermediate phase on non-amenable lattices where bulk order is induced by infinitesimal boundary fields, not spontaneous symmetry breaking.

October 19, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv argues that quantum lattice systems defined on non-amenable geometries — structures such as the infinite Cayley tree, where the boundary grows proportionally to the bulk — can support a form of ordering that has no direct analogue on ordinary Euclidean lattices. The authors study clock models with fewer than five states at coordination number three, employing a boundary-retaining variant of infinite density matrix renormalization group (iDMRG) combined with an exact symmetry-fixing duality transformation.

The central claim is the existence of an intermediate phase sitting between the conventionally ordered and disordered phases. In this regime, the authors report that an arbitrarily weak field applied at the boundary is sufficient to select a specific ordered bulk state — a mechanism they term boundary-sensitive order. Because no spontaneous symmetry breaking in the Landau sense is required, they argue this constitutes a qualitatively distinct ordering principle, one tied to the extensive boundary-to-bulk ratio intrinsic to non-amenable lattices. The result, if confirmed, would represent a bulk-boundary correspondence in which the boundary condition dictates bulk phase selection even in the thermodynamic limit.

These findings are presented as a preprint and have not yet undergone peer review, so independent verification of the numerical methods and the interpretation of the intermediate phase remains pending. The work raises open questions about whether analogous boundary-sensitive phases could appear in other non-amenable geometries or in experimentally accessible systems that approximate hyperbolic lattice structure.
