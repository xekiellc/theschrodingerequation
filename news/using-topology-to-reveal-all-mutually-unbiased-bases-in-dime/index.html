Topology closes 30-year question on MUBs in dimension six

An unreviewed preprint claims to classify all mutually unbiased bases in dimension six, proving no four can coexist.

November 4, 2026

A preprint posted to arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — claims to resolve a problem open for over three decades: whether four mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) can exist in dimension six. MUBs formalize the notion of complementary measurements in quantum mechanics; knowing a system's state perfectly in one basis renders all other mutually unbiased bases maximally uncertain. While dimensions that are prime powers admit complete sets of MUBs, dimension six has resisted classification, with researchers unable either to construct a fourth basis or rule one out.

The authors approach the problem through the topology of complex Hadamard matrices, which encode the transition amplitudes between MUB pairs. Their central topological result is that all order-six complex Hadamard matrices — except for a single isolated matrix due to Tao — form a connected space. Connectedness allows the classification of MUB triples to be reduced to finitely many overlapping four-dimensional regions, which the authors then certify computationally using interval arithmetic. Within each certified region they determine every third basis compatible with any given MUB pair, finding that all triples belong to a two-parameter family or to one isolated triple built from the Fourier matrix. Because none of these triples extend to a fourth basis, the nonexistence of four MUBs in dimension six follows.

The claim is notable in part because OpenAI reportedly announced a separate proof of the same nonexistence result around the same time, suggesting independent convergence on the answer. If the topological argument here survives peer review, dimension six would become the first non-prime-power dimension in which the full MUB structure is completely characterized — a result of direct relevance to quantum information theory, including cryptography and state tomography. Readers should treat the conclusions as provisional until independent verification is complete.
