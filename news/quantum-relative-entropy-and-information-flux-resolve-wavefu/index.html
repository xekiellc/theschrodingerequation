Relative Entropy Framework Proposed to Explain Wavefunction Collapse in QED Waveguides

An unreviewed preprint claims quantum relative entropy governs spatial dephasing and nonlocal collapse in spacelike-separated biphoton waveguide systems.

November 5, 2026

An arXiv preprint — not yet peer-reviewed — proposes that quantum relative entropy, interpreted as an information flux across causal boundaries, can account for how definite measurement outcomes arise from entangled vacuum fluctuations in spacelike-separated regions. The authors construct their framework around dual nonlinear biphoton sources implemented in quantum electrodynamics (QED) waveguides, pumped by an optical frequency comb and seeded by a continuous-wave laser prepared in a coherent state. The central object of study is the single-photon-added coherent state (SPACS), which the authors argue carries a quantum relative entropy that precisely quantifies the entanglement deficit between signal and idler photon fields.

Tracing out the unmeasured idler modes, the paper claims to derive an exact spatial Lindblad master equation along the propagation axis, in which spatial dephasing is driven by what the authors call an "information divergence flux" parameterized by the SPACS overlap fidelity F = |α|²/(1 + |α|²). They then invoke a "quotient measurement theory" — detailed in supplemental material — to decompose nonlocal wavefunction collapse into local coset projections, asserting this resolves apparent nonlocality without permitting superluminal signaling. The authors further claim the framework simultaneously saturates the wave-particle-entanglement triality and provides a basis for time-frequency Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill stabilizer codes anchored to the comb's symplectic structure.

These are substantive claims touching foundational questions in quantum measurement theory and practical implications for continuous-variable quantum error correction. However, as an unreviewed preprint, the derivations and their physical interpretations have not undergone independent expert scrutiny. Readers should treat the results — particularly the assertion of an "exact" resolution of wavefunction collapse — as proposals awaiting rigorous community evaluation rather than established conclusions.
