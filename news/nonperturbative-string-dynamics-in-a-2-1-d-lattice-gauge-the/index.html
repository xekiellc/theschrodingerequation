Superconducting processor probes nonperturbative flux-string dynamics in 2+1D gauge theory

A 63-qubit quantum simulation explores electric flux strings in a Z2 lattice gauge theory beyond the reach of classical tensor-network methods.

November 10, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv reports quantum simulation of a (2+1)-dimensional Z2 lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter on a 63-qubit superconducting processor, 12 of which serve as ancillas. The authors claim to access regimes of string dynamics that classical tensor-network methods struggle to reach, because entanglement growth rapidly limits the timescales those methods can faithfully simulate. The target theory includes an explicit magnetic plaquette interaction, allowing the team to tune between strongly confined and nonperturbative deconfined regimes.

Starting from localized electric flux strings with either static or dynamical endpoint charges, the experiment tracks spatially resolved observables across up to 15 second-order Trotter steps. The authors report signatures consistent with rapid charge and flux spreading in the deconfined phase, enhanced matter occupation inside the string near a pair-creation resonance, and suppressed spreading under strong electric fields. String-path occupations are used to probe transverse rearrangements between configurations related by plaquette operations — dynamics that go beyond simple longitudinal string breaking.

Error mitigation in the experiment exploits the gauge structure directly: local Gauss-law constraints, together with ancilla-based checks and noise rescaling, serve as an experimental resource for detecting and correcting errors rather than merely a theoretical bookkeeping tool. Because this is a preprint that has not yet undergone peer review, the results and their interpretation should be treated with appropriate caution pending independent scrutiny.
