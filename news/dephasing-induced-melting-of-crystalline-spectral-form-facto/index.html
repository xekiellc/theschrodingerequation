Dephasing Degrades Crystalline Spectral Order via Algebraic Bragg Peak Decay

A preprint shows how energy dephasing in open quantum systems erodes sharp spectral Bragg peaks with a characteristic power-law scaling.

November 9, 2026

In isolated quantum systems with highly regular, nearly crystalline energy spectra, the spectral form factor (SFF) displays sharp Bragg peaks at integer multiples of the Heisenberg time — a signature of long-range order in the eigenvalue sequence. This unreviewed preprint, posted to arXiv and not yet peer-reviewed, investigates what happens to that order when such a system is coupled to an environment that introduces energy dephasing.

The authors report that dephasing causes Bragg peaks to fade algebraically rather than abruptly, with peak height scaling as KBragg(p) ∝ (γp)−1/2, where γ is the dephasing strength and p is the revival order. They claim this behavior holds across an extensive intermediate regime, and that the peaks survive up to the coherence lifetime of nearest-neighbor energy spacings even under strong decoherence — a robustness that the paper frames as physically significant.

Beyond characterizing the decay, the authors argue that temporal correlations in spectral fluctuations can serve as a control handle on this order, and that the SFF is a viable diagnostic tool for spectral structure in open quantum systems. These are theoretical claims based on analytical and numerical analysis; independent verification and peer review remain outstanding.
