CV twin-field QKD proposed with √η scaling via Gaussian coherent states

A preprint introduces a continuous-variable twin-field protocol claiming to surpass repeaterless key-rate bounds using coherent-state inputs.

November 10, 2026

An arXiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) proposes a continuous-variable (CV) analogue of twin-field quantum key distribution (TF-QKD), a protocol class notable for achieving key rates that scale as √η with end-to-end transmissivity η rather than the linear η scaling of conventional prepare-and-measure schemes. The authors replace the discrete-variable single-photon encoding of standard TF-QKD with Gaussian-modulated coherent states fed into an untrusted single-photon relay, two ingredients already common in CV-QKD implementations.

The paper presents two reconciliation strategies—full-record reconciliation and a binary phase-disclosure protocol—and claims nearly equal asymptotic key rates under collective attacks for both. For the phase-disclosure variant, the authors assert composable security against general coherent attacks, invoking a bounded test of preparation intensities and a sign-parity complementarity argument. Finite-key analyses incorporating detector inefficiency, dark counts, phase fluctuations, and reference-frame overhead predict rates the authors say exceed both the pure-loss and matched thermal-loss repeaterless bounds for specified apparatus parameters.

If the security proofs withstand peer scrutiny, the work would represent a meaningful extension of the twin-field advantage—long associated with single-photon or discrete-variable encodings—into the Gaussian CV framework, potentially easing hardware requirements for long-distance quantum key distribution. Independent verification of the composability arguments and finite-key bounds will be essential before practical significance can be assessed.
