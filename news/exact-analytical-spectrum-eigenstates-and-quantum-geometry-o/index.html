Quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model yields rare closed-form quantum geometry

Exploiting a hidden sublattice symmetry, researchers derive exact spectra, eigenstates, and geometric tensors for a paradigmatic four-band lattice model.

August 26, 2026

A preprint posted to arXiv — not yet peer-reviewed — reports exact analytical results for the Harper-Hofstadter model at quarter magnetic flux, a four-band tight-binding model describing electrons on a two-dimensional square lattice threaded by a uniform field. The model has recently been realized in ultracold-atom, photonic, and superconducting-circuit platforms, making its theoretical characterization timely. Closed-form solutions for both spectra and quantum geometry are uncommon beyond the simplest two-band cases, so the authors' ability to obtain them here is the central technical achievement.

The key insight is that the quarter-flux Hamiltonian possesses a sublattice symmetry that renders its Bloch Hamiltonian anti-block-diagonal. The authors exploit this structure to diagonalize the system analytically, then derive closed-form expressions for the full quantum geometric tensor (QGT) — encompassing both the Berry curvature and the quantum metric — across all four bands. They also present a general QGT formula for sublattice-symmetric systems expressed in terms of contributions from individual sublattice sectors, which may be applicable beyond this specific model.

Applying their results to fractional-Chern-insulator (FCI) stability criteria, the authors claim to demonstrate analytically that the lowest band is a nearly ideal Chern band — a designation indicating that the Berry curvature and quantum metric satisfy conditions favorable to FCI formation. Because this is an unreviewed preprint, independent verification of the calculations and their implications for experimental platforms remains pending.
