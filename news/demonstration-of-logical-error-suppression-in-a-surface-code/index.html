Surface-code logical error suppression demonstrated on IBM cloud processor

Researchers report sub-threshold logical error suppression in a rotated surface code on IBM's Nighthawk r2, with caveats about long-lived correlations.

October 16, 2026

An unreviewed preprint posted to arXiv claims demonstration of logical error suppression scaling with code distance on a publicly accessible IBM superconducting processor (Nighthawk r2), a key milestone on the road to fault-tolerant quantum computing. Using a rotated surface-code memory, the authors report that increasing the protected distance from 3 to 6 reduces the per-round logical error rate from roughly 0.53% to 0.24% in Z basis and 0.52% to 0.22% in X basis, yielding error-suppression factors of 1.68 and 1.85 respectively under maximum-likelihood decoding.

Central to the result is a suite of circuit-level optimizations: shortened readout windows, fused measurement-and-reset operations, and an XX echo applied to data qubits during ancilla readout. Together these reduce the median per-round logical error rate by factors of 3.8 (X basis) and 1.4 (Z basis) relative to a standard unoptimized extraction circuit. The authors attribute significant performance variation across different physical placements on the chip to qubit-quality heterogeneity, underscoring that device-level variation remains a practical obstacle.

The paper also identifies limitations that complicate a straightforwardly optimistic reading. Over experiments extending to 150 rounds, the distance advantage weakens and long-lived temporal correlations emerge — behavior the authors suggest may reflect a persistent error component not captured by standard depolarizing noise models. The work has not yet been peer-reviewed, and the authors frame their findings as motivation for tighter co-design between processor architecture and quantum error correction circuits, rather than as a definitive demonstration of below-threshold, scalable error correction.
